Subscription Expiry Notification — Requirement Review

The requirement is not yet specific enough to design reliable tests. “About to expire,” “the user,” and “in time” need measurable definitions, and several delivery and eligibility rules are unstated.

Finding Question for the Product Owner
“About to expire” has no defined lead time. It could mean a fixed number of days, a configurable window, or multiple reminders. How long before expiry should the notification be sent? Should there be one notification or a schedule of reminders?
The triggering time and boundary are unclear. “When” could refer to a scheduled time, a date boundary, or a system event. At what exact date and time should the system trigger the notification? What timezone applies, and how should the expiry date itself be interpreted?
“Subscription” is underspecified. Subscription types may have different expiry rules, such as paid, trial, monthly, or annual plans. Which subscription types and statuses qualify? How should trials, paused subscriptions, grace periods, or subscriptions already set to renew automatically be handled?
“The user” may not identify the recipient. An account could have multiple users, an administrator, or a billing contact. Who should receive the notification: the subscriber, account owner, billing contact, all account users, or someone else?
Multiple subscriptions are not addressed. One user may hold multiple subscriptions with different expiry dates. Should each expiring subscription generate a separate notification, or should notifications be consolidated?
“Send a notification” does not specify channel or delivery expectations. It could mean email, SMS, push, an in-app message, or several channels. Which channel or channels should be used? Is successful delivery required, or is an attempted send sufficient? What should happen if delivery fails?
User preferences and eligibility are unstated. Users may have disabled certain channels or opted out of non-essential messages. Should notification preferences, consent, locale, or contactability affect sending? Which rules take precedence?
Changes after scheduling are unhandled. A subscription might be renewed, cancelled, or changed before the reminder is sent. Should the system recheck subscription status immediately before sending and suppress the notification if it is no longer due to expire?
“So they can renew it in time” is not measurable. The requirement does not define how much renewal time is needed or what “in time” means. What is the latest acceptable notification time relative to expiry, and what user action or outcome counts as renewing in time?
Notification content and renewal route are unspecified. The user may need the expiry date, plan details, or a renewal link. What information must the notification include, and should it provide a direct renewal action or link?
Duplicate and repeat sends are not controlled. Retries or repeated processing could send multiple notifications. What is the allowed number of notifications per subscription and reminder window? How should retries avoid duplicates?
Operational edge cases are missing. Delivery may be delayed, the account may be inaccessible, or the subscription may have no valid contact details. What should happen when the notification cannot be sent or the user has no valid contact method? Is the failure recorded or escalated?
